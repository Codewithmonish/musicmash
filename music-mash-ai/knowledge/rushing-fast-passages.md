# Stop rushing fast passages

Rushing is when your tempo speeds up, often in exciting or difficult sections. In a recording
it shows up as a tempo that increases from the first half to the second, or as sections that
run faster than the reference.

## Why it happens
- Nerves or excitement.
- Fast notes that feel uncomfortable, so you try to "get through them".
- Not hearing the beat while focused on the notes.

## Exercise: rhythmic variations
1. Play the fast passage in dotted rhythms (long-short), then reversed (short-long).
2. Then in groups: play four notes, stop on the fifth for a full beat, continue.
3. Play it at the original rhythm with the metronome 10 BPM below target.
4. Record the passage and check whether you land ahead of the click at the start of each bar.

**How long:** 10 minutes on the rushed section, every day for a week.
