# Drone practice for intonation

A tuner shows you a needle; a drone teaches your ear. Play a steady drone on the tonic of your
piece (a drone app, a keyboard with a held key, or a recording) and play against it.

## Why it works
When two pitches are in tune, the sound is smooth. When they are slightly off you hear
"beats": a slow wobble in loudness. The further off you are, the faster the wobble. Your
goal is to make the wobble disappear, which trains you to hear the problem without a screen.

## Exercise
1. Set a drone on the tonic (for a piece in G, a G drone).
2. Play the scale of the piece one note at a time, each note held for four slow beats.
3. On every note, listen for beats. Adjust until the sound is still, then move on.
4. Pay special attention to the 3rd and 7th of the scale; they are the easiest to misplace.
5. Finish by playing the first phrase of your piece slowly over the drone.

**How long:** 10 minutes a day. After a week, check your mean deviation again: most players
see it shrink as their ear starts doing the correction automatically.
