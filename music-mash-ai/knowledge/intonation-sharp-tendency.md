# When you tend to play or sing sharp

A consistent sharp tendency (your average deviation is above zero) usually has a physical
cause rather than a "bad ear".

## Common causes
- **Excess pressure or tension**: pressing strings too hard, squeezing the throat or lips.
- **Excitement and adrenaline**: performance energy pushes pitch upward, especially on
  high or loud notes.
- **Anticipation**: reaching for the next note before the current one is finished.

## Exercise: land low, then centre
1. Pick the three notes that measured the sharpest.
2. For each, deliberately start the note slightly flat, then slide up into the centre while
   watching a tuner or listening to a drone. Do this five times per note.
3. Now play the note directly in the centre with the lightest pressure that still gives a
   clean sound.
4. Play the phrase containing those notes at half speed, with the same light touch.

**How long:** 10 minutes a day for a week. Re-record and compare your mean signed deviation.
