# When you tend to play or sing flat

A consistent flat tendency (your average deviation is below zero) is very common, especially
at the ends of phrases and on descending lines.

## Common causes
- **Running out of energy**: breath, bow speed or finger pressure fades as a note goes on.
- **Descending lines**: players tend to "fall" too far when moving down by step.
- **Fatigue**: pitch sags late in a practice session or a long take.

## Exercise: keep the note alive
1. Hold one note for eight slow beats against a drone. Keep the energy (air, bow, finger
   weight) constant all the way to the end; the last beat should be as centred as the first.
2. Play a descending scale slowly. Think of each step down as "small" and listen for the
   half steps, which are the ones most often made too wide.
3. Play your phrase and add a tiny lift of energy on the last note instead of relaxing it.

**How long:** 10 minutes a day. Check whether the flat notes are mostly at phrase ends; if so,
pair this with the long-note exercises.
